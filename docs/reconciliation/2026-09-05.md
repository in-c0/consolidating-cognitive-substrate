## What it establishes

- **Short-horizon rankings do not predict long-horizon rankings.** Mean Spearman
  ρ(E8, E128) = **0.3354**, CI [0.2517, 0.4146], **all 12 seeds below 0.6**.
- No tested mechanism controls drift: every arm's integrity violation exceeds
  **0.54** at E128.
- Expensive re-grounding buys nothing. `evidence-reconstruction` and
  `provenance-regrounding` each spend ~245 000 reads at E128 and neither beats a
  zero-read heuristic; `provenance-regrounding` is *worse* than free
  `confidence-decay` (0.5721 vs 0.5479) while spending 96% of the ceiling.
- **No delayed-credit policy repairs a culprit belief.** Every arm's raw net
  repair is negative including the winner; the culprit slot still declines. A
  significantly positive `excess_net_repair` means *degrades the state less than
  inaction does*.

Two of seven development findings failed to replicate — both about which cheap
arm holds the frontier at a given horizon. That instability is what H2 predicts,
so the track's development phase demonstrated the effect on itself.

## CCS-C7 — neither promoted nor falsified

## The finding that reaches past its own track

The H2 result is now confirmatory, and it applies to the rest of the programme:

**Every other CCS track runs short streams.** `plasticity-routing` on SDW-1,
`modular-consolidation` on CAMS, `state-promotion` on segmented streams. Their
results stay valid *for their stated horizons* — but none of them licenses a
claim about long-run mechanism **choice**, and this programme's framing is
explicitly about lifetimes.

Adopted as inherited admissibility rule **17**, alongside rule **18** ("better
than doing nothing" is not "it works"), drawn from the B001 arm.

This does not invalidate anything already recorded. It sharpens what those
records mean, and it makes Tier 3 harder rather than easier: a long-horizon
result now exists, and it is unfavourable.
