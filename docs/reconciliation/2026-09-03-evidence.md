## Two admissibility rules adopted programme-wide

EXP-003 exposed a failure mode the programme had not written down.

**Rule 13 — a preregistered success criterion must be satisfiable by some possible
policy.** Preregistration protects against post-hoc reinterpretation; it does not
protect against writing an impossible bar, and that failure is invisible until
results arrive — at which point every available response looks like moving the
goalposts. New preregistrations should demonstrate satisfiability, by naming a
hypothetical policy that would clear the bar, before freezing.

**Rule 14 — a protocol freeze is a provenance claim, not a result.** Now enforced
by the `protocol-frozen` stage rather than left to prose.

Recorded as failure criterion **F9**: a programme can fail because its hypothesis
is wrong, and it can also fail because its protocol asked a question no result
could answer.
