# CCS-C4's second half designed — 2026-09-10

Follow-up to [2026-09-10](2026-09-10.md), which recorded that nobody was
designing the endogenous-commit experiment CCS-C4 needs after EXP-003. Now
someone has. **It is not adopted, not preregistered, not executed, and changes no
claim status.**

Branch `design/exp004-endogenous-commitment` in `plasticity-routing` (`37bb547`);
`main` untouched at `57cb8e5`.

## Why EXP-003 was never going to be enough

EXP-003's own scope section says so: it supports separability from **exogenous**
commitment timing and declines the rest. The reason is structural rather than
cautious — **a clock cannot interact with allocation.** It has no inputs, so
nothing the allocator does can change what it does. Separability from a clock is
close to trivial. Separability from a policy reading the substrate the allocator
just modified is the actual claim.

## The hard part, and why the obvious repair is wrong

With an endogenous commit policy, holding the policy's *function* fixed does not
hold its *behaviour* fixed: allocation changes the substrate that feeds it.
Realized commit counts therefore differ across allocation arms **even at fixed
commit policy**.

EXP-003's load-bearing control — `ACTION_MATCHED_RANDOM`, matching realized
commit volume by construction — **cannot be built here**, because the policy
decides the volume, not the design.

And the obvious repair is a mistake. Matching or conditioning on realized commit
count is **conditioning on a mediator**: commits lie on the causal path from
allocation to outcome, so removing them removes part of the effect being
measured. A design that "controls for commits" without saying so reports a
controlled direct effect as though it were a total one.

## The design's move: information set as the independent variable

The 15-feature decision-time whitelist in `features.py` already partitions along
exactly the needed line:

- **S-features** — stream-derived, recomputable from the prefix alone,
  **allocation-invariant**.
- **X-features** — substrate-derived, **allocation-coupled**. Including
  `pred_error`, which looks like a stream quantity but is `1 - utility(answer, …)`
  where `answer` is read back out of the substrate.

Four rungs: `EXOGENOUS` (clock, the EXP-003 bridge) → `STREAM-ADAPTIVE` (S only,
provably allocation-blind) → `STATE-COUPLED` (S ∪ X) → `LEARNED` (S ∪ X, trained).

**Separability is treated as a function of how much the commit policy can see**,
not as a binary. The ladder locates where it breaks instead of asking whether it
breaks.

## Three tests

- **T1 — oracle-mapping invariance.** Exhaustive search over the 81 mappings per
  rung. No policy involved, nearly free, and the strongest: if the ideal
  allocation changes across commitment rungs, CCS-C4 is false **in the world
  itself** and no policy-level result rescues it.
- **T2 — transplant penalty.** Train the allocator under one rung, evaluate under
  another. Reuses the L5b crossover machinery verbatim — that control already
  trains two policies, pins both by SHA-256 and evaluates the cross without
  retraining.
- **T3 — interaction equivalence by TOST.** Separability asserts an interaction is
  *absent*, and a non-significant interaction is usually underpowered rather than
  absent. **"Inconclusive — underpowered" is declared in advance as a third
  outcome**, so a wide interval cannot be reported as support.

Disagreement between tests is itself a result: T1 holding while T2 fails means
the ground truth is separable but policies entangle — a finding about
*learnability*, not architecture.

## The gate most likely to fire

**G1 — commitment must matter.** If the commitment main effect is null, every
separability test passes vacuously: an allocation effect is trivially invariant
across regimes that are themselves indistinguishable.

SDW-1 was built to make *allocation* matter and has never been asked whether
*commitment timing* does. G1 failing is a live possibility, and the protocol
declares it a legitimate outcome producing **no separability claim in any
direction** rather than a bug to tune away.

## Identifiability — the honest answer

- **Allocation-blind rung: clean.** Inputs are provably invariant across
  allocation arms (asserted mechanically as gate L9), so the operators are
  orthogonal by construction.
- **Coupled rungs: partial.** The total effect is always estimable. The
  controlled direct effect is identifiable **only because this is a simulator**
  with randomized assignment and full observability — the protocol requires that
  sentence to accompany it whenever it is reported.
- **No rung delivers unqualified separability.** A full success supports
  "separable from these four commitment families on SDW-1", which is a large
  advance on "separable from a clock" and still not the unqualified claim.

## Recorded consequences

CCS-C4 gains a fourth falsifier (T1 refuting). CCS-C1 gains one too, since T1
refuting would show the two operators are not separable at ground truth rather
than merely hard to disentangle. Both claims' statuses are **unchanged**.

Also applied: programme rule 17 / CCS-C14. T1 and T2 are ranking comparisons, so
horizon is a reported factor and a single-horizon run may not be described as
separability in general.

## Ownership — flagged, not decided

EXP-004 reaches into COMMIT_INTERNAL, which `state-promotion` owns, though it
imports nothing from that track: commitment here chooses **when** a pending window
promotes, never **which** items. `plasticity-routing` is recommended as host on
continuity grounds — same substrate, world, lock machinery and leakage suite, and
T2 reuses L5b directly — with `state-promotion` notified rather than asked, since
nothing here constrains that track's protocol.

That is a programme-level call and the design says so rather than making it
silently.
