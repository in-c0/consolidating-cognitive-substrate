### Why CCS-C4 was not promoted

CCS-C4 claims allocation is **separable** from COMMIT_INTERNAL: that one can be
varied while a distinct commit policy is held fixed, producing a measurable
difference.

EXP-001 establishes something narrower and genuinely valuable — that **allocation
content matters** under matched write, storage and compute budgets, surviving
capacity matching, privileged task identity, budget-matched random allocation and
cross-world attribution.

But every arm in EXP-001 shares one commitment regime. What varies is *where*
writes go, not *when* durability is decided. **There is no COMMIT_INTERNAL policy
to hold fixed**, so no run of this experiment — however many seeds, however tight
the intervals — could settle separability. The evidence is of the wrong shape, not
of insufficient strength.

Recorded as `scope: partial`, with `establishes` and `does_not_establish` both
populated, and a `held_back_reason` on the claim. **CCS-C4 was not narrowed to fit
the result**, because narrowing it would convert an unanswered question into an
answered one by editing the question.

There is a symmetry worth noting. On 2026-09-02 this ledger recorded that K1
firing *against* a learned router would not falsify CCS-C4, since the claim is
about allocation being a separable lever rather than about the policy being
learned. That same distinction now blocks the promotion: K1 firing *for* the
learned router does not confirm it either. A distinction that cuts both ways is
some evidence it was real.

That track independently reaches a compatible conclusion, preregistering
**EXP-001R** — a replication on 32 untouched seeds — on the explicit grounds that
five seeds and 20,000 bootstrap resamples do not make an effect broadly
established.

## New umbrella capability — partial-scope evidence

Rather than forcing a binary promotion, the ledger now represents partial support
directly.

Evidence entries carry `scope`: `full` or `partial`. A `partial` entry **must**
record `establishes` and `does_not_establish`. Two invariants enforce the rest:

- A claim carrying admissible evidence that is **not** promoted must state a
  `held_back_reason` — the umbrella may not sit on evidence without saying why.
- A **full-scope, supporting, admissible** entry on an unpromoted claim is
  **rejected outright**. Either promote the claim, or record the entry as partial
  and say what it does not establish.

The second rule is the one that matters: it makes under-promotion cost something
too, so "hold everything at unresolved" is not a free default.
