# CCS-C14, EXP-003 scope, and the last sibling — 2026-09-10

Three owner decisions actioned. **The architecture thesis did not advance.**

## 1. CCS-C14 added — and the ledger gained a second axis

The owner made this conditional: add it *only if the umbrella is intended to
carry methodological claims as well as architecture claims*.

**It is, and the answer was already sitting in the repository.** The umbrella
carries **eighteen inherited admissibility rules**, every one a methodological
assertion, none of them tested. Refusing to carry a *confirmatory* methodological
claim while carrying eighteen untested methodological rules would be incoherent.

So CCS-C14 enters at `confirmatory-supported`:

> On LIS-v0, across the nine reference maintenance mechanisms tested, mechanism
> rankings measured at short lifetimes are not reliable proxies for rankings at
> long lifetimes.

Evidence: mean Spearman ρ(E8, E128) = 0.3354, CI [0.2517, 0.4146], p = 0.0001
under Holm, all 12 seed-level ρ below 0.6.

### The problem this created, and the fix

CCS-C14 is the **first claim in the CCS ledger to reach a supported status**. The
headline "0 claims with empirical support" has been this repository's single most
load-bearing sentence for eight days. Letting a methodological result flip it to
"1" would have invited precisely the misreading the umbrella exists to prevent —
a reader concluding the substrate thesis had started to be validated.

Two mechanical guards were added:

**`claim_class`** — every claim is now `architecture` or `methodology`. The two
are counted separately in the validator summary, in the rendered ledger table
(with a `**METHOD**` marker), and in the README. A methodology claim must carry
`advances_synthesis_readiness: false`, and the validator rejects it otherwise, on
the grounds that a claim about how to evaluate mechanisms cannot clear a tier
about whether the substrate works.

The headline now reads:

```
ARCHITECTURE claims with empirical support: 0  <- the substrate thesis is not established
-- methodology claims -- 1 of 1 supported (these do NOT advance synthesis readiness)
```

**`inherited_from`** — a claim entering already at a supported status must name
the pre-existing preregistered hypothesis it descends from, or it is
indistinguishable from one written after seeing the result. The validator now
refuses `pilot-supported` or `confirmatory-supported` without it.

CCS-C14 qualifies because `lifetime-integrity`'s A001 H2 was preregistered
**before** confirmatory execution. Its ledger entry says so explicitly and adds
that this must not become a precedent for admitting post-hoc claims.

Scope is deliberately narrow — LIS-v0 and the nine tested mechanisms. It does not
generalise to all agents or benchmarks, and the ledger should refuse any later
attempt to widen it without its own evidence.

## 2. EXP-003 — scope made binding before adoption

The owner adopts the factorial design conditional on the scope being explicit in
the **preregistration**, not left in the analysis section. Done on
`design/exp003-allocation-commitment-factorial` (`df14a19`); `main` untouched.

§12 of the draft is now titled *BINDING, owner-adopted* and states that a
successful run supports separability of allocation content from **exogenous**
COMMIT_INTERNAL timing — a schedule fixed in advance that does not respond to the
stream — and does **not** establish separability from a learned or adaptive
internal-commit policy. The operator is a clock; allocation could be separable
from a clock while entangled with a policy that decides when to commit based on
what it has seen, and the design cannot distinguish those cases.

The predeclared ledger consequence is now in the protocol: a successful EXP-003
becomes **admissible partial-scope evidence for CCS-C4** and is explicitly not a
route to marking C4 supported. Closing the remainder needs a further experiment
in which the commit policy is itself endogenous.

Recording it pre-result is what makes it a scope declaration rather than a
post-hoc narrowing, and whoever reads out EXP-003 is bound by it.

`ACTION_MATCHED_RANDOM` is confirmed as the load-bearing control, on the owner's
stated grounds: it holds **realized** allocation and commit volume fixed while
changing which items are allocated where. The draft now adds that if that
matching fails at execution (gate V6), the primary contrast does not exist and
**no C4 evidence is produced at all**, partial or otherwise.

## 3. Adaptive Commitment launched — the last sibling

[`in-c0/adaptive-commitment`](https://github.com/in-c0/adaptive-commitment) exists
at `98378e3`. **All five CCS sibling repositories now exist**, eight days after
the umbrella recorded that one of five did.

**Charter only.** No literature audit, no benchmark, no preregistration, no code,
no result. It holds **no claim**, and the umbrella records none against it — the
standing owner decision is that a behavioural-commitment claim is written after
that track's audit defines a falsifiable one. Writing one now would be inventing
a hypothesis on its behalf.

Its DAG node stays at `planned`, not `pre-result-scaffold`: a charter is not a
design.

Three things the charter fixes deliberately:

- **Execution order is gated and mechanically enforced** by its own `make check`:
  audit → falsifiable claim → cost model → benchmark. Nothing below step 2 may be
  built first, because a benchmark written before the claim encodes the claim and
  the audit is then scored against a question the benchmark already assumed.
- **The audit may close the track.** It states that if the question turns out to
  be already answered, that is the result, and the track should report it and
  close rather than build a benchmark to rediscover it.
- **It takes OP-1.** Latency and intervention cost are the resource envelope's
  only `INCOMMENSURABLE` dimensions and currently pin both `ccs` nodes at
  `not-designed`. Ownership is now **resolved**; the problem is not. The charter
  requires any exchange rate to be declared as a choice, with results reported
  across a range rather than at one flattering point.

It is also **not blocked on `state-promotion`**, which makes it the only CCS track
that could produce an admissible result without the state pipeline reading out.
